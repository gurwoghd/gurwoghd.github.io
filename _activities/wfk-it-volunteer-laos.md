## Overview

The WFK IT Volunteer Program is an overseas IT volunteering program. I joined its on-site Laos track in a team of four. Two of us were in charge of ICT, and the other two were in charge of sharing Korean culture. The online Jordan track I joined in the same period is written up [separately]({{ '/activity/wfk-it-volunteer-jordan/' | relative_url }}).

## My Role

I was one of the two ICT members. We designed the ICT lessons, made the slides, and taught in turns with the other ICT member. The two culture members made and gave the Korean culture presentations, and all four of us helped each other with preparation and teaching.

## What I Did

**ICT lessons**

The learners were a mix of university students and working adults, including some who worked in government-related jobs.

- Python syntax, taught through hands-on coding rather than slides alone.
- Data handling and plotting with NumPy, pandas, and Matplotlib.
- Machine learning basics: linear regression and logistic regression, implemented and run in Python with scikit-learn.

**Korean culture sessions**

- Traditional Korean food, cities, clothing, and culture. The culture members presented these, and the whole team helped prepare.

**Cultural exchange**

- Learned about local Laotian culture in return, including learning Laotian dance and eating Laotian food together.

**Tools:** Python, NumPy, pandas, Matplotlib, scikit-learn

## Challenges

**Teaching hands-on machine learning clearly.** Building a model with scikit-learn is easy to do and hard to explain. Working out how to present the coding steps so beginners could follow took a lot of preparation time.

**Language.** I taught in English, but many learners did not speak it well. I used Google Translate to show Lao translations on screen as we spoke, and I explained things to the learners who did speak English so they could pass them on in Lao.

**Pace and level.** Learners often could not keep up with the pace we had planned. Each week we watched how well they were following and adjusted the speed and difficulty. To calibrate, the team compared what had been hard for each of us when we first learned programming, data analysis, and machine learning, and how long it had taken. We used those experiences to set the pace.

## What I Learned

- **Teaching required understanding the math.** We did not need to teach the mathematics behind linear and logistic regression, but we studied it so that we could explain the models properly. That made it clear to me how much machine learning depends on linear algebra, multivariable calculus, and probability and statistics.
- **Knowledge alone is not enough.** Activities like this, and research as well, depend on English and communication skills as much as on technical knowledge. This is something I want to keep improving.
