## Overview

Einstein Class is a volunteer program in which university students are dispatched to rural areas to teach school subjects. I was dispatched to Yeonggwang County, Jeollanam-do, during the winter break of 2024. I taught at Yeonggwang Elementary School and at a community child center called Yeonggwang-ui Bit (영광의 빛).

## My Role

I was a teacher for both sites. At the elementary school I taught a group of six fifth graders, and at the child center I taught one child one-on-one. I taught Korean, English, math, and science, covering the theory as well as hands-on activities.

## What I Did

**Subject lessons**

- Korean, English, math, and science, with explanations of the core concepts rather than only problem solving.
- Korean: we read newspaper articles together, talked through what they meant, and practiced summarizing them.

![A student working through a math workbook]({{ '/assets/images/activity/einstein-class/math-workbook.jpg' | relative_url }})

- Science: I brought science materials, explained the principle behind each one, and then the students built it themselves.

![Students building science kits at their desks]({{ '/assets/images/activity/einstein-class/science-kit-building.jpg' | relative_url }})

**Music video project**

- The students chose the song "Travel" (여행) by BOL4, drew a picture for each part of the lyrics, and then arranged the drawings in order to make a music video.

**Time outside lessons**

- It was winter, so on snowy days I went outside and played with the students.

**One-on-one teaching at the child center**

- At Yeonggwang-ui Bit I worked with one child who had an intellectual disability, with a learning level below their age group, and taught them one-on-one.
- I also took the child outside to play with the other children at the center, such as playing on the playground and flying a drone, so that they could get along with the group.

![Children at the child center doing a cooking activity around a long table]({{ '/assets/images/activity/einstein-class/child-center-cooking.jpg' | relative_url }})

## Challenges

**Very different levels in one class.** The six fifth graders were not at the same level. Some had been taught by their parents early on, and one had won a prize in a math competition. Others had little interest in studying. For the strong students I prepared extra explanations and materials so that they could go further, and I tried to bring out as much of their potential as I could.

**A student who would not attend.** One student had no interest in studying and often did not come to class, sometimes not attending at all. I contacted the student's parents separately, and I also called the student aside to explain what it means to show up and take part. I did scold the student, but afterward I calmed down and explained things patiently.

**Helping a child fit in at the center.** The child I taught at the center had a hard time getting along with the other children. I thought being able to mix with the group mattered more than studying, so I sometimes set the lesson aside and went out to play with them. One day, during a soccer game, the child ran off with the ball, probably wanting attention. It backfired, and the other children disliked them even more. When I scolded the child afterward, they became very upset. I stopped them, held them, and slowly talked through why they had run off, what they were feeling, and what they could do next time. The next day the child came up to me by themselves and apologized.
